Job Market Paper

Learning Tail Features by Random Forest: with Applications to Health Economics Datasets Draft available upon request
Abstract

This paper develops Pareto-GRF, a nonparametric estimator of conditional tail exponents using a Pareto approximation and honest generalized random forests. I establish consistency, pointwise asymptotic normality, and feasible variance estimation, separating forest localization bias from finite-threshold approximation bias. An application to the top one percent of medical expenditures in the 2016–2023 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey documents heterogeneity in tail severity associated with age and cancer diagnoses, conditional on exceeding a high spending threshold.

Working Papers

A Simple and Powerful Diagnostic Test for Binary Choice Models [arXiv]
with Ting Ji, Laura Liu, and Yulong Wang
Abstract

Conventional probit and logit models impose thin-tailed latent errors, an assumption with implications for estimation rates and inference. We develop a diagnostic that identifies the tail class of the unobserved error from extreme observations of a heavy-tailed covariate conditional on the binary outcome. The procedure uses a fixed number of upper order statistics and requires neither model estimation nor a parametric link specification. Under mild regularity conditions, the resulting invariant test nearly attains the point-optimal power envelope. We extend the method to short panels with unobserved individual effects without estimating incidental parameters. Simulations evaluate finite-sample size and power, and an application to firm innovation decisions rejects the thin-tailed restriction underlying standard binary choice specifications.

Detecting Limited Overlap from Covariate Tails Draft available upon request
with Ting Ji, Laura Liu, and Yulong Wang
Abstract

Lack of overlap can lead researchers to trim observations with extreme propensity scores, changing the target estimand. We derive a tail-index criterion for regular identification of the average treatment effect: under regularity conditions, the treated and control covariate tail indices must differ by less than a factor of two. The criterion determines when root-n estimation and conventional Gaussian inference remain available. We develop a diagnostic test based on subsample tail-index estimators, avoiding propensity-score estimation, and establish its pointwise asymptotic size. The framework accommodates overlap failures both in covariate tails and at finite interior points. In firm data, conventional trimming excludes the largest firms, which hold three fifths of total assets, while the diagnostic does not reject regular identification.
